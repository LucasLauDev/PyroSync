1. Data Requirements
- Integrate active fire hotspot data from MODIS and VIIRS satellites.
- Use NASA Earthdata FIRMS as a potential source of satellite hotspot data.
- Include historical MODIS observations from the early 2000s to the present.
- Incorporate VIIRS observations to provide continuity and more detailed hotspot information.
- Include recent hotspot data to support updated fire activity monitoring.
- Store or process hotspot records with relevant information, such as:
  - Geographic coordinates (latitude and longitude).
  - Detection date and time.
  - Hotspot or fire detection information.
  - Sensor or satellite origin (MODIS or VIIRS).
- Support historical and recent hotspot data within a consistent time series.
2. MODIS and VIIRS Data Harmonization
- Develop a harmonization process to integrate MODIS and VIIRS hotspot records.
- Account for differences between the two satellite sensors, including:
  - Spatial resolution.
  - Detection sensitivity.
  - Observation schedules and frequency.
- Produce a consistent dataset that allows MODIS and VIIRS records to be analysed together.
- Ensure that the sensor of origin does not affect the interpretation of overall fire activity patterns, trends, and anomalies.
- Enable comparisons between historical and recent hotspot observations.
3. Area of Interest (AOI) Selection
- Allow users to select a specific geographical area for fire activity analysis.
- Provide interactive geographical selection capabilities.
- Support selecting an area through:
  - Drawing an area or boundary directly on the map.
  - Entering latitude and longitude coordinates.
- Retrieve hotspot records within the selected area.
- Allow users to analyse fire activity within their chosen geographical boundaries.
4. Date Range Selection and Filtering
- Allow users to select a specific date range for hotspot analysis.
- Support historical analysis from the early 2000s to the present.
- Filter hotspot records based on the selected:
  - Geographical area.
  - Start date.
  - End date.
- Update the calendar, statistics, and visualizations according to the selected area and date range.
5. Burning Activity Calendar
- Develop an interactive burning activity calendar based on harmonized hotspot data.
- Display the number of detected hotspots for each month and year.
- Cover historical records from the early 2000s to the present.
- Use colour ramps to represent the number of detected hotspots.
- Allow users to identify:
  - Months with the highest fire activity.
  - Years with the highest fire activity.
  - Seasonal burning patterns.
  - Periods with unusually high or low fire activity.
  - Changes in burning activity over time.
- Make it possible to compare fire activity across different months and years.
- Ensure that the calendar updates according to the selected area and date range.
6. Historical Analysis and Anomaly Detection
- Provide historical fire activity comparisons for the selected area.
- Calculate historical averages or medians of hotspot activity.
- Identify unusual fire activity compared with historical patterns.
- Compare the current month's hotspot activity against the historical median.
- Highlight periods with significantly higher or lower hotspot activity.
- Allow users to examine changes in fire frequency and intensity over time using hotspot observations.
- Help users identify critical periods and potential changes in regional fire patterns.
7. Data Analytics and Statistics
- Provide key numerical indicators and statistics for the selected area.
- Display the total number of detected hotspots.
- Present fire activity across different months and years.
- Identify the months and years with the highest hotspot counts.
- Provide historical comparisons, including anomalies against multi-year averages or medians.
- Present relevant analytical results through visualizations.
- Ensure that all statistics are based on the selected geographical area and date range.
8. Interactive Map
- Provide an interactive map to display geographical information.
- Allow users to explore their selected area of interest.
- Support geographical area selection through map interaction.
- Allow users to identify hotspot locations within the selected area.
- Provide an option to enter latitude and longitude coordinates to define an area of interest.
9. Data Export and Download
- Allow users to download hotspot records from their selected search.
- Provide hotspot data in CSV format.
- Ensure downloaded records correspond to the selected geographical area and date range.
- Allow users to download relevant analytics and visualizations.
10. User Interface and Accessibility
- Develop a web application with an interactive and accessible interface.
- Make the application suitable for the intended users:
  - Early warning and emergency responders.
  - Scientists and researchers.
  - Land managers.
  - Firefighters.
- Use accessible colour ramps to represent differences in hotspot activity.
- Ensure that visualizations make historical patterns, seasonal trends, and unusual fire activity easy to identify.
- Present important statistics and analytical results clearly.
- Make the application easy to use for geographical and historical fire activity exploration.
